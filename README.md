An engineer interview measures what the candidate can build. An engineering manager interview measures what a team builds while the candidate is responsible for it. The loop therefore adds three subjects that an engineer loop never grades: decisions about people, delivery through other people, and judgment about what a team should not do.

This repository is a short, free guide to that loop. It explains each round, lists the questions that are commonly reported, and gives one-page reference sheets for preparation. Worked answers and full lessons are in the course.

## The loop at a glance

Most companies run three stages. The details vary by company, and the table below describes the commonly reported shape.

| Stage | Length | Who runs it | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | 30 minutes | Recruiter | Team size managed, level fit, reasons for moving |
| Hiring manager screen | 45 to 60 minutes | The future manager | Management approach, one or two people stories, technical background |
| Onsite rounds | 4 to 6 rounds of 45 to 60 minutes | Managers, engineers, a product partner | People management, project retrospective, system design, behavioral, and sometimes coding |

## FAQ

### What is asked in an engineering manager interview?

The questions fall into five groups: people management, project delivery, behavioral, situational, and technical. People management carries the most weight, because it is the one subject that separates a manager from a senior engineer. The [question bank](questions/README.md) lists about 70 questions across these groups.

### Do engineering managers get coding rounds?

Some do. Companies with a strong engineering culture often keep one coding round for managers of small teams, usually at a lower difficulty than the engineer loop. Director-level loops rarely include coding. The [coding round page](rounds/coding.md) explains how to find out and how much to prepare.

### What is a project retrospective interview?

It is a round in which the candidate presents one past project for 15 to 20 minutes and then answers detailed questions about its decisions. The interviewer is measuring ownership, technical depth, and the ability to describe mistakes precisely. See the [project retrospective page](rounds/project-retrospective.md).

### Can I interview for a manager role without management experience?

Yes, although the evidence must come from somewhere. Leading a project of three or more engineers, mentoring, running hiring interviews, and handling a conflict between peers are all accepted as evidence. The [role comparison sheet](cheat-sheets/ic-vs-manager.md) shows how to present that experience.

### How long does it take to prepare for an engineering manager interview?

About four weeks at six hours a week is enough for a candidate who already manages a team. A first-time manager candidate should allow six weeks, because the story bank takes longer to build. The [four-week plan](cheat-sheets/four-week-plan.md) gives the schedule.

### How is the manager loop different from a staff engineer loop?

A staff engineer loop measures technical decisions that affect many teams. A manager loop measures the people and delivery decisions inside one or more teams. The sibling repository [staff-engineer-interview](https://github.com/design-gurus/staff-engineer-interview) covers the staff loop.
