# Questions to Ask the Interviewer

Most rounds end with five minutes for the candidate's questions. For a manager candidate these minutes are graded, because the questions show what the candidate pays attention to. A good question is specific, concerns the team or the way decisions are made, and could not be answered from the company website. Prepare three per interviewer, and expect to ask two.

## Questions by interviewer

### The hiring manager

- "What would you want this team to have delivered six months after I start?"
- "Which of the team's problems is a people problem, and which is a technical one?"
- "How many of the current team members have been here longer than two years?"
- "How do you prefer to hear bad news from your managers?"
- "What did the previous manager of this team do well, and what would you like done differently?"

### A peer manager

- "How do managers here resolve a priority conflict between two teams?"
- "How does the performance review process work, and how much of your time does it take?"
- "What do you wish you had known in your first three months?"
- "How is headcount decided, and how often does it change during the year?"

Headcount is the number of positions a manager is approved to fill.

### An engineer on the team

- "What is the most frustrating part of getting a change into production?"
- "What did your best manager do that you would want from the next one?"
- "How much of the on-call load is caused by one or two systems?"
- "When did the team last say no to a request, and what happened?"

### A product partner

- "How do you and engineering decide what is in scope for a quarter?"
- "Can you describe a recent disagreement with engineering and how it was settled?"
- "What do you need from the engineering manager that you are not getting today?"

### A director or senior leader

- "What are the two outcomes this organization must achieve this year?"
- "How do you decide between hiring managers from outside and promoting from inside?"
- "What would make you say, after one year, that this hire was a mistake?"

## What each kind of question signals

| Question about | Signals that you care about |
|---|---|
| Outcomes expected in six months | Delivery and accountability |
| Team tenure and attrition | The health of the team you would inherit |
| How conflicts are resolved | How decisions are really made |
| Release and on-call problems | Engineering quality |
| The previous manager | Continuity for the team |

Attrition is the rate at which people leave a team.

## Questions to avoid

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| "What does the company do?" | It shows no preparation |
| Pay, level, or promotion timing, asked of an interviewer | These belong in the recruiter conversation |
| "Did I do well?" | The interviewer cannot answer it |
| A long question that is really a statement about yourself | It uses the interviewer's time without asking anything |
| No questions at all | It reads as low interest in the team |

## How to use the answers

Write down what each interviewer says. Three uses follow:

1. A later interviewer may ask what you have learned about the team so far.
2. Two different answers to the same question show where the organization disagrees.
3. The answers are your best evidence for deciding whether to accept an offer.
