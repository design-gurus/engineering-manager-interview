# Engineer and Manager Roles Compared

An individual contributor (IC) is an engineer who is measured on the work they produce themselves. An engineering manager is measured on what their team produces and on how the people in it develop. The interview for a first manager role tests whether the candidate understands that change and has already begun to work that way.

## What changes

| Area | Individual contributor | Engineering manager |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of output | Your own code and designs | The team's delivered results |
| Main working tool | The editor | Conversations, written plans, and decisions |
| Time horizon | The current task or sprint | The quarter and the year |
| Feedback speed | Minutes, from tests and reviews | Weeks or months, from team results |
| Technical role | Makes the technical decision | Makes sure a good decision is made, and by the right person |
| People responsibility | Mentoring, by choice | Hiring, reviews, growth, and exits, by obligation |
| Calendar | Long blocks of focused work | Many short meetings |
| Success sounds like | "I built it." | "The team shipped it, and two people grew in the process." |

## What interviewers fear in a first-time manager

| Fear | How to answer it with evidence |
|---|---|
| Will keep writing the hardest code personally | A case in which you handed a difficult task to someone else and supported them |
| Will avoid hard conversations | A case in which you gave critical feedback to a peer |
| Wants the title, not the work | A clear reason for managing that mentions other people's growth |
| Has never been responsible for delivery | A project you led with three or more engineers and a date |
| Cannot hire | The number of interviews you have conducted and one hiring decision you influenced |

## Turning IC experience into manager evidence

A candidate without the title still has usable evidence. The table shows how to state it.

| What you did as an IC | How to present it |
|---|---|
| Led a project as tech lead | "I led five engineers for four months, set the milestones, and reported status to the director." |
| Mentored a junior engineer | "I met with her weekly for six months, and she took over the service's on-call rotation alone." |
| Ran interviews | "I conducted about 40 interviews and trained three new interviewers." |
| Resolved a design dispute | "Two engineers disagreed on the storage choice. I had each write a one-page proposal, and we decided in one meeting." |
| Covered for your manager | "I ran planning and one-on-ones for six people during a three-month leave." |

A tech lead is an engineer who directs the technical work of a project without having direct reports.

## Questions asked of IC-to-manager candidates

- "Why do you want to manage?"
- "What will you miss about engineering, and how will you handle that?"
- "How would you manage someone who was your peer last month?"
- "How will you stay technical without writing most of the code?"
- "Tell me about a time you led without authority."

A strong answer to the first question takes 30 seconds and contains one real moment: "The most useful thing I did last year was helping two engineers reach the next level. I want that to be my main job."
