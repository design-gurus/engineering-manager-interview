# The Story Bank

A story bank is a short list of real cases from your career, prepared in advance and mapped to the kinds of questions an interviewer asks. Ten stories are enough for a full manager loop of five rounds. With fewer than eight, the same story is repeated to different interviewers, and they notice when they compare notes.

## The ten stories to prepare

| # | Story | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A low performer who improved | The gap in numbers, your plan, the result |
| 2 | A person you let go or moved out | The process, the timeline, what you told the team |
| 3 | A top performer you grew | The larger scope you gave them and the outcome |
| 4 | A hire you are proud of, or one that failed | The evidence you used to decide |
| 5 | A conflict between two people | How it reached a decision |
| 6 | A project that was late or failed | Your share of the cause |
| 7 | A project that succeeded | The team's result and your specific part |
| 8 | A disagreement with your manager or a peer | Your evidence, and what you did after the decision |
| 9 | A mistake of your own | The cost and the change you made |
| 10 | A change you introduced to how the team works | A before and after measure |

Every column should have at least two marks. A column with one mark is a gap, and the next story you add should fill it.

## One card per story

Write each story on a card of about 120 words with six fields.

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Title | "Search migration slipped six weeks" |
| Context | Team of nine, five-month plan, two dependent teams |
| Your decision | Cut two integrations at week ten and moved the date once |
| Numbers | 11 of 30 items done at week six; shipped six weeks late; error rate fell from 2.1 to 0.4 percent |
| Lesson | Review dependency estimates in week one |
| Hard follow-up | "Why did you not see it at week three?" |

The last field matters. Preparing the hardest follow-up question for each story removes most of the surprises in the loop.

## Rules for using the bank

- Assign stories to rounds before the onsite, so that no story is used twice.
- Reserve story 6 or 7 for the [project retrospective](../rounds/project-retrospective.md), because that round needs the most depth.
- Keep every story within the last five years where possible.
- Remove names and details that would identify a former report.
- Practice each story aloud until it takes between two and three minutes.
