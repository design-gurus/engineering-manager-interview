# Red Flags in an Engineering Manager Interview

A red flag is an answer pattern that makes an interviewer doubt the candidate, regardless of the rest of the answer. Manager loops are sensitive to them, because a poor manager affects every person on a team. The twelve patterns below are the ones that interviewers commonly report, each with a correction.

## People

| # | Red flag | What the interviewer concludes | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blaming a former report, peer, or manager | Will blame this team as well | Describe behavior and your own part in the outcome |
| 2 | No case of a hard conversation | Avoids conflict | Prepare one case of critical feedback and one exit |
| 3 | Waiting months before acting on low performance | Lets problems grow | State how many days passed before you raised it |
| 4 | Speaking of people as resources to be assigned | Does not see individuals | Mention one person's goals in at least two stories |
| 5 | Every story ends in success | Is not being honest, or has not been tested | Include one case in which the outcome was poor |

## Ownership

| # | Red flag | What the interviewer concludes | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | "We" for every action | Cannot tell what the candidate did | Use "I" for your decisions |
| 7 | "I" for every result | Takes the team's credit | Name who did the work |
| 8 | A failure with no personal cause | Does not learn from failure | Say which decision of yours contributed |

## Judgment and technical depth

| # | Red flag | What the interviewer concludes | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | "I would let the team decide" for every technical question | Has lost technical depth | Give your own view first, then say how you would involve the team |
| 10 | Solving the hardest problem personally in every story | Has not made the move from engineer to manager | Show one case in which you delegated the hard part |
| 11 | Answering a situational question without asking anything | Acts on assumptions | Ask one or two clarifying questions first |
| 12 | No numbers anywhere | The results may not be real | Put one number in each Situation and each Result |

## The same answer, with and without flags

**Question:** "Tell me about a project that failed."

**With flags 1, 6, and 8:**

> "We had a migration that failed because the platform team kept changing their interface. We tried everything, but they were not cooperative."

**Corrected:**

> "I led a migration for a team of six that we stopped after ten weeks. The platform interface changed twice, and I had planned as if it were stable. I should have asked for a frozen version in week one. I now get a written commitment on any dependency before I give a date."

The corrected answer is no longer than the first. It replaces blame with one decision the candidate owned and one change in practice.

## How to review your own answers

1. Record yourself answering five questions from the [question bank](../questions/README.md).
2. Listen once, and mark every flag by its number.
3. Rewrite only the sentences that were marked.
4. Record the answers again two days later.

Most candidates find three or four flags in the first recording, and flags 6 and 12 are the most frequent.
