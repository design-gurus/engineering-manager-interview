# The Behavioral Round

A behavioral question asks about something the candidate actually did: "Tell me about a time when..." The round rests on one assumption, which is that past behavior predicts future behavior. For a manager, the interviewer is looking for a decision that affected other people, the trade-off behind it, and a measured outcome for the team.

## How it differs from the people management round

| | Behavioral round | People management round |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | The candidate's own conduct | Decisions about a specific report |
| Typical topics | Failure, deadlines, disagreement with a superior, mistakes | Performance, hiring, firing, delegation |
| Usual interviewer | A manager from another group | A peer manager |

Many companies combine the two in one hour. Prepare for both as if they were separate.

## The eight question families

| Family | Example question | What a manager answer must include |
|---|---|---|
| Leading through difficulty | "Tell me about leading a team through a hard period." | What you told the team, and when |
| Rejected proposals | "Describe a proposal of yours that was turned down." | How you responded after the decision |
| Project failure | "Tell me about a project that failed." | Your share of the cause |
| Tight deadlines | "Describe a deadline you could not meet as planned." | What you cut, and who agreed to it |
| Difficult team member | "Tell me about a difficult person on your team." | The behavior, the conversation, the result |
| Workplace mistake | "Tell me about a mistake you made." | The cost, and the change you made afterward |
| Explaining to non-experts | "Explain a technical decision you made to a non-technical audience." | How you checked they understood |
| Proud achievement | "Which project are you most proud of?" | The team's result, and your specific part in it |

The full list is in [Behavioral questions](../questions/behavioral-questions.md).

## Answer structure

Use STAR, which stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Give the Situation and Task 30 seconds together, the Action about 90 seconds, and the Result 30 seconds. A manager answer then adds one sentence on what changed in the way the candidate manages. See [STAR for managers](../cheat-sheets/star-for-managers.md) for a full sample.

## A worked example

**Question:** "Describe a deadline you could not meet as planned."

- **Situation and task:** "My team of six had ten weeks to deliver a billing export for an enterprise customer. In week four, an estimate for one integration grew from two weeks to five."
- **Action:** "I listed three options with their costs: move the date by three weeks, drop two of the five export formats, or add two engineers from another team. I took the options to the product lead and the customer's account manager on the same day. We dropped two formats and kept the date."
- **Result:** "We delivered on the original date with three formats. The remaining two shipped five weeks later, and the customer renewed."
- **What changed:** "I now ask for an estimate range on every integration, and I review the widest ranges in week one."

The answer works because the manager made a choice among stated options, involved the right people on the same day, and gave numbers.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a "failure" that was really a success.
- Talking for five minutes before reaching the action.
- Giving a result without a number.
- Telling the same story for three different questions. Prepare eight to ten stories with the [story bank](../cheat-sheets/story-bank.md).
