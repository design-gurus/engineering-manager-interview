# The Engineering Manager Interview Loop

An engineering manager loop has three stages: a recruiter screen, a hiring manager screen, and an onsite of four to six rounds. The whole process commonly takes three to six weeks. Each stage answers a different question about the candidate, and knowing that question tells you what to prepare.

The details below describe the commonly reported shape. Every company adjusts it, so ask the recruiter for the exact list of rounds.

## Stage 1: Recruiter screen

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Length | 30 minutes |
| Question it answers | Does the candidate's scope match the open role? |
| What is asked | Team size, number of direct reports, years of management, reasons for moving |

A direct report is a person whose performance review the manager writes. Recruiters compare this number with the role, so state it exactly: "seven direct reports, all engineers, for two years".

## Stage 2: Hiring manager screen

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Length | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Question it answers | Would I trust this person with one of my teams? |
| What is asked | Management approach, one performance story, one delivery story, technical background |

Prepare three stories for this call: a low performer you managed, a project that was late, and a technical decision you influenced. Most hiring managers ask for two of the three.

## Stage 3: Onsite rounds

| Round | Typical interviewer | Weight for a first-line manager |
|---|---|---|
| [People management](people-management.md) | A peer manager | Highest |
| [Project retrospective](project-retrospective.md) | A senior manager or director | High |
| [System design](system-design-for-managers.md) | A senior or staff engineer | High |
| [Behavioral](behavioral.md) | A manager from another group | Medium |
| [Situational](situational.md) | Often combined with behavioral | Medium |
| [Coding](coding.md) | An engineer | Low to medium, and absent at many companies |
| Cross-functional | A product manager or designer | Medium |

A first-line manager is a manager whose reports are all individual contributors. The cross-functional round is a conversation with a partner from product or design, and it measures how the candidate handles priority disputes.

## How the decision is made

After the onsite, each interviewer writes feedback independently, and the group meets to compare it. Two patterns cause most rejections at this stage:

1. **Thin people evidence.** The candidate described team outcomes but never described a decision about one person.
2. **Technical doubt.** The engineers on the panel could not tell whether the candidate understood the systems the team owned.

A candidate who is strong on delivery and weak on these two areas is often offered a lower level, or a senior engineer role, instead of a rejection.

## How the loop changes by level

| Level | What is added |
|---|---|
| First-line manager | Coding round is more likely; design round stays close to the engineer version |
| Senior manager | Questions about managing other managers and planning across two or three teams |
| Director | Organization design, budget, and strategy questions replace coding |
