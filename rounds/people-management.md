# The People Management Round

The people management round asks how the candidate has handled decisions about individual people: a weak performer, a strong performer, a hire, a conflict. It carries more weight than any other round, because it is the only one that a senior engineer cannot pass on technical skill alone. A strong answer names one person's situation, the decision the manager made, and what happened to that person and to the team afterward.

## What the interviewer measures

| Signal | What it sounds like in an answer |
|---|---|
| Acts early | "I raised it in our next one-on-one, nine days after the first missed deadline." |
| Is specific about expectations | "I wrote down three outcomes for the next six weeks and we both signed off on them." |
| Separates the person from the performance | The answer describes behavior and results, never character |
| Follows a fair process | Feedback was documented, and the human resources partner was involved before any formal step |
| Accepts hard outcomes | The candidate can describe letting someone go without blaming them or avoiding the subject |

A one-on-one is a regular private meeting between a manager and one report, usually 30 minutes each week.

## The six question families

| Family | Typical question | The decision the interviewer wants to hear |
|---|---|---|
| Low performers | "Tell me about someone who was not meeting expectations." | When you acted, and how you defined improvement |
| Firing | "Have you let someone go?" | How you knew improvement was not coming |
| Hiring | "How do you decide a team needs another person?" | The evidence you used, and what you rejected |
| Delegation | "How do you decide what to hand off?" | The match between the task and the person's growth |
| Top performers | "How do you keep your best engineer engaged?" | The larger scope you gave them, and the risk you accepted |
| Conflict | "Two engineers disagree and the team is split. What did you do?" | How you got to a decision without choosing a favorite |

The full list is in [People management questions](../questions/people-management-questions.md).

## A worked example

**Question:** "Tell me about a time you managed a low performer."

A strong answer has five parts, and it takes about three minutes.

1. **The gap, in numbers.** "A mid-level engineer finished 4 of 11 planned tasks over two sprints, and two of the four were returned in review."
2. **The diagnosis.** "I asked before assuming. He had moved from front-end work to a payments service and had not asked for help."
3. **The plan.** "We agreed on three outcomes for six weeks, a weekly check-in, and a pairing partner for the first two weeks."
4. **The result.** "By week six he was completing 8 or 9 tasks out of 10, and review returns stopped."
5. **What changed in how I manage.** "I now schedule a check-in at week three for anyone who changes technical area."

The fifth part is the one most candidates omit, and it is the part that shows the manager learned something reusable.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a policy ("I believe in regular feedback") instead of one case.
- Presenting every story as a success. Interviewers expect at least one case in which the person left.
- Using "we" for decisions that belonged to the manager alone.
- Revealing details that would identify a former report.
