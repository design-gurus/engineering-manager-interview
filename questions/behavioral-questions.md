# Behavioral Interview Questions for Engineering Managers

These 18 questions ask about the candidate's own past conduct. They are grouped into six families, and each family states what the interviewer is measuring. Every question should be answered with one real case, told in about three minutes, with a number in the result.

## Failure and mistakes

**Measuring:** whether you take your share of the cause and change something afterward.

1. Tell me about a project that failed.
2. Tell me about a mistake you made as a manager.
3. Describe a decision you would reverse if you could.
4. Tell me about feedback you received that was hard to hear.

## Deadlines and pressure

**Measuring:** whether you make a trade-off openly instead of asking the team to work longer.

5. Describe a deadline you could not meet as planned.
6. Tell me about leading a team through a difficult period.
7. Describe a time you had to deliver with fewer people than you asked for.

## Disagreement with superiors and peers

**Measuring:** whether you argue with evidence, and whether you support a decision after losing.

8. Tell me about a proposal of yours that was rejected.
9. Describe a time you disagreed with your manager.
10. Tell me about a decision from senior leadership that you had to carry out and did not agree with.

## Difficult people

**Measuring:** whether you describe behavior instead of character, and whether you acted directly.

11. Tell me about a difficult team member.
12. Describe a peer manager you found hard to work with.
13. Tell me about a time you gave feedback that was not well received.

## Communication

**Measuring:** whether you adapt the message to the audience and check that it was understood.

14. Explain a technical decision you made to a non-technical audience.
15. Tell me about delivering bad news to your team.
16. Describe a time a misunderstanding caused a problem. How did you correct it?

## Achievement

**Measuring:** whether you can separate the team's result from your own contribution.

17. Which project are you most proud of, and why?
18. Tell me about a time you raised the standard of your team's work.

## How interviewers follow up

Expect two or three follow-up questions after every answer. The most common ones are below, so prepare them for each story.

| Follow-up | What it tests |
|---|---|
| "What exactly did you do, as opposed to the team?" | Your personal contribution |
| "What would you do differently?" | Whether you learned anything |
| "How did the other person see it?" | Whether you can state another view fairly |
| "What happened afterward?" | Whether the result lasted |
| "How did you measure that?" | Whether the number is real |

## Example of a short, complete answer

**Question 8:** "Tell me about a proposal of yours that was rejected."

"I proposed moving my team of seven from two-week releases to daily releases, and I estimated five weeks of work on the pipeline. My director rejected it because a compliance audit was eight weeks away. I asked what evidence would change the decision after the audit, and she named two measures: rollback time and failed deployments. I spent those eight weeks collecting both. Rollback took 40 minutes on average, and 3 of 14 releases had failed. I presented the numbers again after the audit, and the proposal was approved for the following quarter."

The answer is 100 words, and it contains the rejection, the response, and a measured follow-up.
