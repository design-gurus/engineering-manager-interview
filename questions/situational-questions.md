# Situational Interview Questions for Engineering Managers

These 16 questions describe a hypothetical situation and ask what the candidate would do. They are grouped by the people involved, and each group states what the interviewer is measuring. Answer each one with the five steps from [the situational round](../rounds/situational.md): clarify, name what matters, act now, act later, and link to experience.

## Mistakes and quality

**Measuring:** whether you disclose a problem quickly and protect the people involved from blame.

1. You discover a defect in a shipped feature that nobody else has noticed. What do you do?
2. An engineer on your team causes a two-hour outage by skipping a review step. How do you respond?
3. You realize that an estimate you gave your director last week was wrong by a month. What do you do?

## Unfamiliar ground

**Measuring:** whether you can lead without being the expert, and how you learn quickly.

4. You are given a team that works in a technical area you have never worked in. Describe your first 30 days.
5. Your manager assigns you a project and you do not know where to start. What do you do?
6. You inherit a team whose previous manager was well liked. How do you begin?

## Customers and stakeholders

**Measuring:** whether you can refuse a request with options, and stay calm under pressure.

7. A customer demands a feature in four weeks, and your team estimates twelve. What do you do?
8. An angry customer escalates a complaint about your team to your vice president. How do you handle it?
9. The product manager adds three requirements halfway through a project. What is your response?
10. Two stakeholders give your team conflicting priorities. How do you resolve it?

A stakeholder is anyone outside the team who depends on its work or can change its priorities.

## The team

**Measuring:** whether you weigh the individual, the team, and the business together.

11. Your best engineer tells you she has another offer. What do you do in the next 48 hours?
12. An engineer reports inappropriate behavior by a coworker. What are your next steps?
13. Two senior engineers refuse to work together. What do you do?
14. Half of your team asks to work on a new project, and the current one is not finished. How do you respond?

## Organization and change

**Measuring:** whether you can carry out a decision you did not make, and communicate it honestly.

15. Your headcount is cut by two with no change to your commitments. What do you do?
16. A reorganization moves your team under a new director with different priorities. How do you manage the first month?

## A worked answer in outline

**Question 7:** "A customer demands a feature in four weeks, and your team estimates twelve."

| Step | Answer |
|---|---|
| Clarify | "Which part of the feature does the customer need on that date, and why that date?" |
| Name what matters | The customer relationship, the team's existing commitments, and the quality of what ships |
| Act now | Ask the team which part can be delivered in four weeks. Present three options to the account owner: a reduced version in four weeks, the full version in twelve, or the full version in eight with one other project delayed |
| Act later | Agree on a check-in every week with the customer, and record the trade-off in writing |
| Link | One sentence on a similar negotiation and its result |

The answer never says only "no" and never says only "yes". It converts an impossible demand into a choice among options with stated costs.
