# Project and Delivery Interview Questions

These 18 questions cover how a manager plans, tracks, and delivers work through a team. They are asked in the project retrospective round, in the hiring manager screen, and in conversations with product partners. Each group states what the interviewer is measuring.

## Planning and estimation

**Measuring:** whether your plans rest on evidence, and whether you plan for being wrong.

1. How do you estimate a project that has many unknowns?
2. Walk me through how you planned your last large project.
3. How do you decide what goes into a quarter and what is left out?
4. Tell me about an estimate that was badly wrong. What did you change afterward?

## Scope and priorities

**Measuring:** whether you can cut work deliberately and explain the cut to others.

5. Tell me about a time you reduced the scope of a project to meet a date.
6. How do you prioritize when everything is described as urgent?
7. How do you balance new features against technical debt?
8. Describe a project you stopped. How did you decide?

Technical debt is the future cost of work that was done quickly instead of well.

## Tracking and risk

**Measuring:** whether you learn that a project is late early enough to act.

9. How do you know whether a project will meet its date?
10. Tell me about a project that slipped. When did you learn of it, and what did you do?
11. What risks did you identify at the start of your last project, and which ones occurred?
12. How do you report status to your own manager?

## Cross-team delivery

**Measuring:** whether you can deliver work that depends on people who do not report to you.

13. Tell me about a project that depended on another team that had different priorities.
14. Describe how you work with your product manager when you disagree on scope.
15. Tell me about a time you had to escalate. How did you do it?

To escalate means to bring a decision to a more senior person because the people involved cannot settle it.

## Process and quality

**Measuring:** whether you improve how the team works and can show the effect.

16. What process did you change on your team, and how did you measure the result?
17. How do you handle an incident in production, during it and afterward?
18. How do you keep quality high when the team is under schedule pressure?

## What a strong delivery answer contains

| Element | Weak version | Strong version |
|---|---|---|
| Size | "A large project" | "Nine engineers across two teams for five months" |
| Plan | "We planned carefully" | "Four milestones, each with a demo and a go or no-go decision" |
| Signal | "We realized we were behind" | "At week six we had completed 11 of 30 planned items" |
| Decision | "We worked hard to catch up" | "I cut two integrations and moved the date by two weeks, with the product lead's agreement" |
| Result | "It was a success" | "Shipped two weeks late, and support tickets on that flow fell from 120 to 35 a month" |

The strong column takes no more time to say. It only requires that you collect the numbers before the interview.

## Follow-up questions to prepare

- "What did the plan look like in week one, and how did it differ at the end?"
- "Who objected to that decision?"
- "What would you have done with two fewer engineers?"
- "How did the team feel about the change?"
